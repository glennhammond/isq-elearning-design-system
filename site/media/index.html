Imagery & media

Digital learning imagery and media standards

Media is used to orient, explain, demonstrate and humanise learning — never merely to fill space. Remove the asset mentally: if the learning is unchanged and it contributes no useful context, omit it.

Image taxonomy

TypePurposeTypical useAvoid
Hero / openerEstablish place, tone and major transition.Course covers, section openers.Embedded titles, edge-critical subjects.
Context imageGround an explanation in a real environment.Split cards, image-and-text.Generic stock unrelated to content.
Scenario imageSupport a consistent decision-maker perspective.Scenario stages, evidence reveals.Changing characters or style mid-scenario.
Evidence / objectShow a document, device, location or artefact.Forms, emails, policy documents.Tiny text or inaccessible detail.
Diagram / modelExplain relationships, processes or systems.Frameworks, pathways, comparisons.Dense infographic posters.
Interface screenshotShow a specific digital action or state.Software guidance, labelled graphics.Whole-screen captures with unreadable text.

Photography and editorial direction

Contemporary Australian schools, workplaces and professional-learning contexts. Natural or soft directional light. Authentic professional behaviour, restrained expression. Clear subject hierarchy with room for responsive crop. Stable locations, subject scale and treatment throughout a scenario.

Sensitive subject and child-safety imagery

  • Students are usually shown from behind, in silhouette, partially obscured or out of focus.
  • No distress performance — avoid visible fear, injury, crying, confrontation.
  • Prefer the principal, teacher or decision-maker viewpoint.
  • Avoid students entirely in the most sensitive legal, abuse or consequence sections.
  • Sensitive assets require explicit learning-design and stakeholder review before release.

Dimensions, ratios and safe areas

CanvasPrimary useFormat
1920 × 1080 (16:9)Course covers, heroes, full-width photography, videoJPEG/WebP; MP4 for video
1600 × 1000 (8:5)Diagrams, labelled graphics, carouselsPNG for diagrams; JPEG for photography
1200 × 900 (4:3)Image-and-text, split componentsJPEG
1200 × 800 (3:2)Cards, galleries, timelinesJPEG, consistent crop across a set
1200 × 1200 (1:1)Profiles, flashcards, quiz optionsJPEG or transparent PNG
Safe-area rule: central 60–70% for covers/backgrounds, 80% for cards, 90% for inline images. Reserve ~40–50% clear negative space where text will overlay an image.

Video and audio

1920×1080 preferred (1280×720 acceptable where bandwidth requires it). Concise segments, clear chaptering, minimal decorative transitions. Accurate synchronised captions always; a transcript where it supports access, search, review or reuse.

Accessibility

Media typeMinimum requirement
Informative photographAlt text communicates instructional purpose, not every visible detail.
Decorative imageMarked decorative or empty alt text where supported.
Complex diagramConcise alt summary plus adjacent written explanation or accessible table.
VideoAccurate captions; transcript where appropriate.
Describe media as produced to support accessibility and tested against the agreed checklist — never an unqualified compliance claim without an audit.
